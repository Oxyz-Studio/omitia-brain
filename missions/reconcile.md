# Mission: reconcile coverage and meaning

## Objective

Repair a gap between what sources contain, what ingestion records claim, and what the brain actually remembers. Restore missing context where possible and leave unresolved gaps explicit and recoverable.

This mission is useful after a truncated import, lost cursor, connector migration, interrupted run, concurrent edit, deleted source, or discovery that an earlier summary omitted the context needed by a later statement.

## Context and authority

Read the brain entry point, governance, affected source registrations, ingestion records, previous receipts, and the notes that depend on the suspect material. Define a bounded source range or subject to reconcile. Use the same source access and editing permissions as the relevant ingestion policy.

## Working brief

- Compare actual evidence with recorded coverage. Separate material never fetched, fetched only partly, fully considered but intentionally excluded, integrated, and blocked from integration.
- Search by stable source identity, date range, participants or entities, and reference-note links as appropriate. Consult historical and superseded material when it may explain a current reference; keep it out of current factual answers unless properly labeled.
- Retrieve enough surrounding material to resolve dependencies. A later statement such as “we chose the second offer” requires the earlier alternatives or another reliable source identifying them.
- If the meaning can be recovered, repair the reference note and affected summaries, then correct the processing record. Retain the provenance connecting the recovered knowledge to the evidence.
- If a past record overstated success, correct it transparently. A previous receipt is evidence about a run, not proof that the content was understood.
- If the source is gone, apply retention and access policy before seeking an alternative. Keep the supported portion and mark the unsupported conclusion or missing dependency.
- Resume incomplete work from explicit gaps. An overlap is preferable to silent omission when collection boundaries are uncertain; recognize already integrated versions to avoid duplicate notes.

Do not reconstruct missing facts from plausibility. Do not interpret an unavailable item as irrelevant or silently convert it into “no durable change.” An unresolved gap can be left open, or deliberately abandoned by an authorized policy decision with the consequence recorded.

## Completion evidence

Leave a receipt that identifies the suspected gap, the source range actually re-examined, recovered context, corrected notes or records, unresolved dependencies, and the next safe action.

Where recovery changes a conclusion, record what was corrected and which summaries now reflect it. Verify that the normal retrieval path reaches the repaired meaning.

## Stop and recover

Respect the mission budget. If the required history is too large or unavailable, preserve a clear checkpoint and the smallest useful next search. Do not continue without bounds or change retention rules to make the problem disappear.

When a missing piece blocks only one conclusion, leave that conclusion unresolved and complete unrelated authorized work. Notify according to the established reporting policy.
