# Mission: recover usable knowledge

## Objective

Recover selected knowledge and continuity after loss or damage, or verify a chosen recovery path through an isolated rehearsal. Show what can actually be used again and what remains missing.

## Context and authority

Read the current policy, affected mission and source records, recovery configuration, available history or copies, and prior incident or rehearsal receipts. Define the affected scope, intended recovery point, destination, allowed changes, and evidence needed for success.

Use existing authority for the requested recovery. A rehearsal uses an isolated destination; it does not authorize replacement of live files or changes to services. If the needed authority or copy is missing, record the exact dependency and complete independent inspection. Apply current access, deletion, and retention rules before restoring material.

## Working brief

- Inspect what happened and what remains intact. Separate lost source evidence, damaged canonical knowledge, missing receipts, an outdated derived index, and unavailable runtime access.
- Select a permitted version or copy with its covered scope, exclusions, and known limits. Do not assume the latest copy is sound or that version history includes separately retained evidence and configuration.
- Preserve the current recoverable state before an authorized replacement. Compare versions and reconcile other contributors' changes; avoid a broad rollback when the damage is confined to a candidate or one update.
- Restore the bounded material to the chosen destination. Rebuild derived indexes only when needed and allowed. Keep old permissions, retired jobs, and deleted data from being revived against current policy.
- Compare recovered notes, policy, evidence locators, receipts, and incomplete items. Inspect dependencies that were stored elsewhere or require access that the new environment lacks.
- Ask a representative future question through the normal entry or search path. Verify meaning, dates, qualifications, authority, and supporting evidence. A checksum or readable archive listing alone is insufficient proof of a usable restore.

## Completion evidence

Save a receipt naming the incident or rehearsal, recovery source and version, restored scope, destination, checks actually performed, retrieval result, remaining loss or uncertainty, and next action. Distinguish restoration to an isolated destination from authorized return to live use.

If source coverage cannot be reconstructed, leave it unresolved and route it to [reconciliation](reconcile.md). Do not invent missing content or claim an untested part of the brain was restored.

## Stop and recover

Respect the configured budget and preserve a safe checkpoint. If live use, service changes, or new access needs a separate authority, leave the recovered result reviewable and identify that dependency. Do not automatically resume schedules merely because their files were recovered.
