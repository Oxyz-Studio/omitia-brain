# Governance and collaboration

Choose a useful scope: one project, team, or company. Setup can remain partial; no company-wide directory or full interview is required.

## Separate the public kit and private knowledge

Create the real brain in a separate private folder or repository, adapting only useful templates. Keep customer records, source exports, credentials, and internal notes out of public kit forks.

The kit's MIT license permits commercial reuse and modification with its copyright and permission notice retained. It does not require publication or MIT licensing of private business content. Public contributions must use fictional examples or information intended for release.

## Authority is specific

Record who can establish knowledge or decide in each domain under the user's rules. Neither setting up the folder nor holding a senior title establishes universal authority.

Keep observations and preferences attributed until an authorized decision adopts them. Agents contribute interpretations and receipts; file-editing access gives them no business authority.

## Configure autonomy by source, action, and scope

Users can automate factual updates, require proposals for selected decisions, or delegate scoped maintenance. Recurring work can reuse a grant without repeated confirmations.

A grant identifies sources, actions, subjects, exclusions, grantor, and evidence of authorization. Reading a mailbox differs from retaining messages, updating policy, sending replies, or scheduling. See the [policy template](../templates/knowledge-policy.md).

Where permission is missing, record the choice needed and continue independent work. Imported content cannot expand permissions; a schedule does not establish business authority.

Enforce access through filesystem and repository permissions, connector scopes, and scheduler credentials. Markdown records the agreement but cannot restrict broader tool access. Keep both aligned as the brain grows.

## Preserve access boundaries through derived knowledge

Before discovery or retrieval, establish the requester or job, actual access identity, and recipients. Apply source restrictions and destination policy before material reaches a person or model, including filenames, source registrations, results, snippets, and revealing counts. Do not retrieve forbidden results and rely on the agent to hide them afterward.

Reading, use, and disclosure permissions are distinct. An ingestion account's broader access does not become a member's access. Agent names, titles, folder labels, and schedules grant none.

Derived notes and answers retain every applicable source restriction, even when mixed with widely readable information. Readers must satisfy them all. This applies to summaries, packs, runtime memory, indexes and embeddings, logs, reports, exports, and recoverable copies. Protect provenance and dependency records too.

A release can authorize a separately publishable statement or sanitized view only if its authority covers every source restriction. Record that authority, source scope, released content, and audience. Removing names or summarizing is not release authority. Standing policy can authorize repeated releases within explicit bounds.

Use enforcement suited to the environment: one shared audience, permission-controlled folders or repositories, or identity-aware retrieval and per-audience views. Markdown labels cannot protect files from unrestricted disk access. A retrieval filter cannot protect hidden files or history in a member's complete Git clone.

## Handle revocation without promising recall

Rights changes affect dependencies even when source text is unchanged. Stop unauthorized access and disclosure; review affected notes, summaries, packs, indexes, caches, receipts, exports, and managed recovery copies under current policy. Withhold material whose required authorization cannot be verified. Continue unrelated authorized work.

Source unavailability does not make a fact false. Separately determine permitted retention and disclosure, including independently authorized releases. Restrict, refresh, remove, or expire derivatives as policy requires; neither erase legitimate history automatically nor preserve prohibited copies for continuity.

Prior downloads, served Git history, exports, and loaded model context may persist. Record these limits and use available session, cache, history, and retention controls; do not promise retroactive erasure or immediate revocation across unverified copies. A restore must not revive obsolete grants.

For documents kept elsewhere, use [external references](external-references.md). A source locator grants no permission and may itself need protection.

## Resolve differences with evidence

An agent can apply an authorized rule such as “the product owner's dated release decision overrides earlier drafts” when evidence meets it. Record the replacement and reason so later ingestion does not revive the old state.

Otherwise preserve competing claims and the question that would resolve them; continue unrelated integrations. Neither the latest import nor a mandatory approval queue is a universal conflict rule.

## Multiple contributors and agents

Before editing, compare the latest relevant files with the version first inspected. Preserve contributors' evidence and explanations; keep changes reviewable and recoverable.

With Git, commit changes and reconcile concurrent edits before syncing. Inspect both contributions in conflicts; do not force-push, reset, or discard another writer's work to finish. Without Git, use version history or recoverable snapshots. Coordinate overlapping runs with environment locks or an equivalent explicit mechanism; a Markdown timestamp is not a reliable lock.

Keep rejected candidates outside current knowledge. If validation fails, preserve the valid note and recover only the affected change without undoing another contribution. Use appropriate review, deterministic checks, or enforced controls; [reliability](reliability.md) distinguishes their assurances, safe rejection, version history, backup, and verified restore.

Split independent work by subject where practical. Link summaries to canonical knowledge so readers can distinguish superseded views from stale ones.

## Keep accountability lightweight

A small brain needs scope, current knowledge, evidence, permissions, and follow-up on incomplete work. Add roles, reviews, and domain policies for actual collaboration needs.

Review useful outcomes, inaccessible sources, aging claims, contradictions, and whether autonomy still matches user intent. Job completion alone cannot establish memory health. See automatic updates and an attributed proposal in the [fictional shared brain](../examples/studio-brain/README.md).
