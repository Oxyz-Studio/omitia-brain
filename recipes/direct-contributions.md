# Recipe: direct contributions

A person can build or enrich the brain by talking to an agent. They can contribute a complete company overview, correct one fact, describe a project, or add a decision. No connector or completed questionnaire is required.

## Start from the person's intent

Read the existing brain entry point and policy before adding to it. If the brain is new, establish the scope, useful first outcome, and permitted actions with the information the person is willing to provide now.

Let the person give a complete briefing or work within one branch of the company. Ask only the questions needed to avoid a misleading result. Make unknowns explicit and preserve a useful continuation if they skip a question or stop.

Do not turn adaptive setup into a mandatory sequence of small steps. Conversely, do not demand company-wide details before helping with one project.

## Preserve the contribution's meaning

Capture the contributor's role or identity at the level needed by policy, the contribution time, its scope, and the distinction between observation, preference, proposal, and decision. Do not invent a contributor identity when the environment does not provide one.

Useful clarifications include:

- Is this already true, a plan, or an idea to consider?
- Does it apply to this project, this team, or the whole company?
- Is it replacing an earlier fact, and from which effective date?
- Is there a document or decision record that supports it?

Ask these when the answer matters; do not present them as a form that every person must complete. Incomplete information can still produce useful knowledge with an explicit uncertainty.

## Work with shared knowledge

Search existing notes before creating or updating a reference. A second contributor can add knowledge without repeating the initial setup.

For a supported correction, update the reference and affected summaries under the current policy. Preserve why it changed and the earlier claim when history is useful. If two contributions conflict, keep their sources and scopes visible; resolve only when evidence or the governance policy supports doing so.

A person's preference does not automatically become company policy. An agent's proposal does not become the user's decision merely because the user continued the conversation. Use the user's explicit meaning and the brain's authority rules.

Read the current note before writing in a shared folder. If another contributor edited it, merge the meaning or leave a clear conflict instead of replacing their work with an older version.

## Make the outcome visible

For a small contribution, use a proportionate durable record: source or contribution locator, observation time, notes affected, remaining uncertainty, and the outcome. There is no need to create a full transcript archive if that is unnecessary or disallowed.

Tell the contributor what became lasting knowledge and what remains unresolved. Execute actions within standing permissions without asking for the same approval repeatedly.

Example: “For the Harbor project, we chose the annual plan effective November 1” can update a project decision. “I prefer annual billing” is a personal preference until more context supports a broader interpretation. These are fictional examples.
